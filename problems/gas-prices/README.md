# Gas and energy prices

## The issue

Gasoline costs have jumped: pump prices in September 2026 were about a third higher than a year
earlier, and home heating oil rose even faster. More than half of what a gallon costs is crude oil,
whose price is set on a world market, so it swings with supply problems at oil fields, refineries and
pipelines that no household can control. Most workers still drive alone to work, so a price spike lands
straight on the commute. Low-income households are hit hardest: they spend about three times as large
a share of their income on home energy as other households, and close to a fifth of their income once
car fuel is added.

## The solution

**Goal: no household should have to spend more than a set, affordable share of its income to heat its
home and get to work.**

1. **States and utility regulators** should cap low-income customers' energy bills at a share of
   income, with arrears forgiven over time for on-time payments, as Ohio and Illinois do.
   ([evidence](solutions/percentage-of-income-utility-bills.md))
2. **Congress and state energy offices** should fund weatherization to clear waiting lists, since it
   cuts bills for good and its savings exceed its cost.
   ([evidence](solutions/weatherization-assistance-program.md))
3. **Congress, states and utilities** should keep emergency bill help steady and paid out on time, and
   make it easy to find through one phone line or website.
   ([evidence](solutions/apply-for-energy-bill-help-this-week.md))
4. **Employers** should cut the commute itself with remote or four-day schedules where the job allows,
   vanpools and pre-tax transit benefits. ([evidence](solutions/employer-commute-cost-cuts.md))

## What we know

- **Crude oil is most of the pump price.** In May 2026, when regular gasoline averaged $4.48 a gallon,
  crude oil made up 52% of the price, refining 22%, distribution and marketing 15%, and taxes 12%. The
  federal gasoline tax is 18.4 cents a gallon; state taxes and fees averaged 33.55 cents as of January
  2026. Because taxes are cents per gallon, their share shrinks as prices rise.
  ([EIA Gasoline and Diesel Fuel Update](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/);
  [EIA, factors affecting gasoline prices](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/gasoline/factors-affecting-gasoline-prices.php))
- **Prices follow crude oil and supply shocks.** The Energy Information Administration says retail
  gasoline prices are mainly affected by crude oil prices, tend to rise in spring and peak in late
  summer, and can jump when something disrupts crude supplies, refineries or pipelines.
  ([EIA, price fluctuations](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/gasoline/price-fluctuations.php))
- **Fuel prices rose sharply this year.** From September 2025 to September 2026, gasoline prices in the
  Consumer Price Index rose 34.2% and fuel oil 74.2%, against 3.7% for all items. Electricity rose 4.4%
  and piped natural gas 5.0%. Regular gasoline averaged $4.33 a gallon in the week of October 12, 2026.
  ([BLS, 2026](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/prices-for-gasoline-increased-34-2-percent-over-the-year-ended-september-2026.htm);
  [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/))
- **Home energy takes three times the share of a low-income budget.** The Department of Energy puts the
  average energy burden of low-income households at 6% of income, three times the roughly 2% for other
  households, and above 30% in some places. ACEEE found two in three low-income households pay more
  than 6% of income on utility bills, and two in five pay more than 10%.
  ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/scep/slsc/low-income-community-energy-solutions);
  [ACEEE](https://www.aceee.org/energy-burden))
- **Adding car fuel, it's close to a fifth of income.** Low-income households spend 17.8% of their
  income on home energy bills and transportation fuel combined, and three in four spend more than 12%.
  Rural and suburban residents who live far from jobs and services rely on cars for long trips.
  ([ACEEE, 2024](https://www.aceee.org/blog-post/2024/05/low-income-households-spend-nearly-20-income-home-energy-and-auto-fuel-costs))
- **Most commuters drive alone.** In 2022, 68.7% of U.S. workers drove alone to work and 15.2% worked
  from home, up from 5.7% in 2019. ([Census Bureau, 2024](https://census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/travel-to-work-since-pandemic.html))
- **Bill help is limited and its timing uncertain.** Congress allocated $4.015 billion for the Low
  Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) for fiscal 2026. About $3.6 billion went to states on
  November 28, 2025, and the last $421 million on April 20, 2026, after delays. The administration's
  fiscal 2027 budget request proposes ending the program.
  ([American Public Power Association, 2026](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/remaining-fy-2026-liheap-funds-disbursed-states-liheap-again-faces-elimination-fy-2027))
