# Get help with energy bills this week: LIHEAP, utility payment plans and shutoff protections

## Summary

If heating or electric bills are more than you can pay, federal money is already waiting at your state
or tribal energy office. The Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) pays part of heating or
cooling bills and helps in an energy crisis, and states set rules that limit shutoffs. A free federal
referral line tells you where to apply. Applying early matters, because programs pay out until the
money runs out.

## How it works

- **Find where to apply.** The federal government doesn't take LIHEAP applications. You apply through
  your state, tribe or territory. The National Energy Assistance Referral (NEAR) service is free and
  tells you where to apply; contact it through the LIHEAP Clearinghouse page linked below, giving your
  city, county and state. USA.gov's energy bills page also links to every local LIHEAP office.
- **Gather papers.** Offices usually ask for recent utility bills, proof of income for the household
  (such as pay stubs or benefit letters) and proof of address. Requirements vary by state.
- **Apply.** Some offices take applications online; others want you in person. LIHEAP can help with
  heating or cooling bills and with emergencies such as a shutoff notice or an empty fuel tank.
- **Ask about shutoff rules.** Each state sets when a utility may disconnect electricity or gas for
  nonpayment, and the rules can depend on the weather, your age, a disability and your utility. USA.gov
  links to the LIHEAP Clearinghouse list of state disconnection rules.
- **Ask your utility for a payment plan or income-based rate.** In some states, low-income customers
  can pay a fixed share of income instead of the full bill (see the
  [percentage-of-income plan](percentage-of-income-utility-bills.md)).
- **Ask for weatherization at the same time.** Households that qualify for LIHEAP may also qualify for
  free home energy upgrades (see [weatherization](weatherization-assistance-program.md)).

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** Congress sets LIHEAP funding each year; for fiscal 2026 it allocated $4.015 billion,
  released to states in two parts, in November 2025 and April 2026. Releasing the full amount on time,
  before the heating season, lets states open applications on schedule. States can simplify the
  application, accept proof of other benefits as proof of income, and share one application across
  LIHEAP, weatherization and utility discount programs.
- **People and companies with means:** Utilities, charities and community foundations run fuel funds
  that cover gaps when LIHEAP money is late or runs out. Employers can add energy bills to emergency
  grant programs and tell workers about LIHEAP during open enrollment. Donors can give to a local fuel
  fund or community action agency before winter.
- **You, this week:** Search "LIHEAP" plus your state name, or use the NEAR referral service, and start
  an application now with your latest utility bill and proof of income. If you already have a shutoff
  notice, say so: crisis help is usually faster. Then call your utility and ask what payment plans or
  discount rates it offers. Never pay anyone a fee to apply: LIHEAP does not charge for benefits.

## Who it helps and what it costs

- Helps low-income households that heat with gas, electricity, oil or propane, including renters
  whose heat is in their name.
- Costs the applicant nothing. The federal cost is set by Congress each year, $4.015 billion in fiscal
  2026, shared out to states.
- Help can arrive in weeks; crisis help is usually faster. The amount varies by state and often covers
  only part of a winter's bills.

## Trade-offs and risks

- LIHEAP money is limited, so many eligible households get nothing once a state's funds are used up.
- Late federal releases delay help; in fiscal 2026 the last 10% reached states only in April, after
  the heating season. The administration's fiscal 2027 budget request proposes ending the program,
  so future funding depends on Congress.
- A one-time payment doesn't fix a bill that is too high every month; that takes a
  [percentage-of-income plan](percentage-of-income-utility-bills.md) or
  [weatherization](weatherization-assistance-program.md).
