# Predatory lending, fees and scams

People with little money get drained by the products that are easiest to reach when cash runs out:
payday and title loans, overdraft and junk fees, rent-to-own, stacked buy-now-pay-later plans,
high-interest credit, aggressive debt collection, betting apps, and get-rich-quick or crypto schemes.
The founder put it as people "being preyed upon in the markets".

## In people's words

Public posts, lightly trimmed, no names.

> Because of an automatic payment on a loan I took, I lost my grocery money and I'm $8 overdrafted.

> I had to get a $200 payday loan to have enough. Used all my money.

> Another day, another overdraft fee and I still have two more bills to pay.

## What we know

- **Payday loans are mostly used for ordinary bills, and they repeat.** About 12 million Americans use
  payday loans each year. The average borrower takes out eight loans of $375 a year and spends about
  $520 on interest, and 69% used their first loan for a recurring expense like rent, utilities or food
  ([Pew, 2012](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/reports/2012/07/19/who-borrows-where-they-borrow-and-why)).
  The CFPB found over 80% of payday loans are rolled over or followed by another loan within 14 days
  ([CFPB, 2014](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/201403_cfpb_report_payday-lending.pdf)).
- **High-cost borrowing is concentrated among people with the least.** In 2025, 7% of U.S. adults used
  a payday, pawn, auto title or tax refund anticipation loan, rising to 13% of adults with income under
  $25,000 and 14% at $25,000 to $49,999
  ([Federal Reserve, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-credit.htm)).
- **Overdraft fees fall on a small group, over and over.** 12% of adults with a bank account paid an
  overdraft fee in the past year
  ([Federal Reserve, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-banking.htm)).
  A typical fee is about $35, and roughly 9% of accounts generate 79% of overdraft and NSF fees. A 2024
  CFPB rule that would have capped overdraft fees at large banks at $5 (or a cost-justified amount) was
  overturned by Congress in 2025 (P.L. 119-10) before it took effect
  ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IN12513.html)).
- **Buy now, pay later is now used for food, and payments are being missed.** 16% of adults used BNPL
  in 2025. 26% of users paid late, one in five used it for groceries or food delivery, and 11% had a
  BNPL payment trigger an overdraft or NSF fee
  ([Federal Reserve, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-credit.htm)).
- **Online sports betting is linked to worse household finances.** A study of credit records for about
  4.4 million adults found that in states that allowed online sports betting, the likelihood of personal
  bankruptcy rose 25% to 30% in the years after legalization, and average amounts sent to collections
  rose 8% ([UCLA Anderson Review, on Hollenbeck, Larsen and Proserpio](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/as-states-permitted-online-sports-gambling-citizens-personal-financial-health-suffered)).
- **Reported fraud losses hit a record, led by investment fraud.** Consumers reported $15.9 billion in
  fraud losses to the FTC for 2025, with $7.9 billion lost to investment fraud, the largest category
  ([FTC, 2026](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-testifies-joint-economic-committee-agencys-efforts-combat-fraud)).
- **Federal payday-loan payment protections are in limbo.** The surviving part of the CFPB payday rule
  (lenders must stop trying to withdraw payments after two failed attempts) took effect on March 30,
  2025, but the CFPB said it would not prioritize enforcing it, and its 2026 regulatory agenda plans to
  reconsider the rule ([National Law Review, July 2026](https://natlawreview.com/article/cfpbs-2026-regulatory-agenda-tees-long-awaited-nprm-reconsider-payday-lending-rule)).

## Open questions

- Which states have measurably cut payday and title loan costs without leaving people with no credit at
  all, and what did borrowers use instead?
- How much do rent-to-own and stacked BNPL plans really cost compared with a credit union loan?
- Which employer programs (small-dollar loans repaid through payroll, earned wage access) save workers
  money, and which add fees of their own?
- What works to stop aggressive debt collection on debts people don't owe, or can't verify?
- What would help someone already caught in betting or an investment-fraud pitch, before the money is
  gone?
