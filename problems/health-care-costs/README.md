# Health care costs

## In people's words

> I've spent years doing everything I could possibly do and I still can't afford my own medical bills or my own home.

> Been in a constant state of extreme anxiety and dread lately but I can't afford my meds without insurance right now

> I lost my insurance when I got laid off and the flu shot is $90.

## What we know

- **People are skipping care.** In a KFF survey from May 2026, 28% of adults said they skipped or postponed care they needed in the past year because of cost. Among uninsured adults under 65 it was 62%. ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/issue-brief/americans-challenges-with-health-care-costs/))
- **And skipping medicine.** In a KFF poll from late February and early March 2026, 43% of adults said they had cut prescription costs in the past year: 27% didn't fill a prescription, and 19% cut pills or skipped doses. That was up from 33% in 2025. ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/issue-brief/americans-challenges-with-health-care-costs/))
- **26.7 million people (7.9%) had no insurance for all of 2025**, about the same as 2024, according to the Census Bureau report released September 15, 2026. Job-based insurance covered 53.5% of people for at least part of the year, so losing a job often means losing coverage. ([Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/demo/p60-291.html))
- **Marketplace help shrank in 2026.** The extra ("enhanced") premium tax credits ended after 2025. KFF estimates that subsidized enrollees who kept the same plan saw their premium payments rise 114% on average. KFF projects that average paid Marketplace enrollment could fall to about 17.5 million in 2026, down from 22.3 million in 2025. In a KFF survey, 9% of 2025 enrollees had become uninsured. ([KFF, Feb 2026](https://kff.org/affordable-care-act/aca-marketplace-enrollment-is-down-in-2026-but-all-of-the-data-isnt-in-yet); [KFF, May 2026](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/what-we-know-so-far-about-2026-aca-marketplace-enrollment-premiums-and-deductibles/))
- **Keeping a work plan after a layoff costs a lot.** With COBRA, a former worker can be charged up to 102% of what the plan costs. ([U.S. Department of Labor](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/health-plans/cobra)) In 2025 the average job-based plan cost $9,325 a year for one person and $26,993 for a family. Workers paid on average $1,440 and $6,850 of that, and the employer paid the rest. ([KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey 2025](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/report/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/))
- **Medicaid rules are changing.** Under the 2025 reconciliation law, states must make adults in the Medicaid expansion group meet work requirements starting January 1, 2027. Some states are starting earlier. (KFF tracker, updated September 29, 2026: [KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/medicaid-work-requirements-tracker-1115-waivers))
- **Low-cost clinics are carrying more of the load.** Community health centers served 32.7 million patients in 2025, and 17% of them had no insurance. Dental care made up 12% of visits. KFF reports that federal health center funding expires in December 2026. ([KFF, Oct 2026](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/issue-brief/community-health-center-patients-financing-and-services))
