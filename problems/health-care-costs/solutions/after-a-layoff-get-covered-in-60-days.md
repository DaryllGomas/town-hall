# Lost your job? You have 60 days to get new coverage

## Summary

Losing job-based insurance opens a Special Enrollment Period. You can sign up for a Marketplace plan within 60 days of losing coverage, or in the 60 days before it ends, without waiting for open enrollment. Medicaid takes applications all year. Savings are based on your expected income for the year, not on whether you have a job, so a drop in income after a layoff can make you eligible for help you didn't get while working.

## How it works

1. **The clock starts when coverage ends.** HealthCare.gov says you may qualify for a Special Enrollment Period if you lost job-based coverage in the past 60 days or expect to lose it in the next 60 days. You may need documents showing the loss, such as a letter from your employer or plan.
2. **One application checks everything.** A Marketplace application tells you whether you qualify for a private plan with savings, Medicaid or CHIP, and it shares your information with your state's Medicaid agency if you might qualify. You can apply for Medicaid and CHIP any time of year.
3. **Income, not job status, sets your savings.** HealthCare.gov says savings and eligibility are based on household size and estimated income for the year you need coverage, not on employment status. Estimate honestly, including severance and unemployment pay, and update it when you find work.
4. **Compare with COBRA.** COBRA lets you keep your old plan, but you can be charged the full premium plus a fee, up to 102% of the plan's cost (Department of Labor). In 2025 the average job-based plan cost $9,325 a year for one person and $26,993 for a family, most of it paid by the employer until then (KFF). COBRA can still make sense if you're in the middle of treatment or have already met this year's deductible.
5. **Watch the COBRA trap.** If you take COBRA and later drop it or stop paying on your own, that doesn't open a new Special Enrollment Period. Your COBRA running out at the end of its term does.

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** States can send Marketplace and Medicaid information with every unemployment claim and every layoff notice, and let people apply for coverage on the same site. Medicaid work requirements for expansion adults begin January 1, 2027, in most states (some states started earlier, KFF). States can make sure people who recently lost a job know which exemptions and grace periods apply to them.
- **People and companies with means:** Employers planning layoffs can give every departing worker a one-page sheet with their coverage end date, the 60-day deadline, a COBRA price next to an estimated Marketplace price, and time on the clock to meet a free enrollment helper. Foundations can fund certified enrollment assisters at unemployment offices and job centers.
- **You, this week:**
  1. Find your exact coverage end date from HR or your plan, and put the date 60 days later on your calendar.
  2. Go to HealthCare.gov (or your state's Marketplace if it runs its own) and fill out an application with your expected income for the year. See what plans, savings or Medicaid you qualify for before you decide on COBRA.
  3. If you need help, use the HealthCare.gov "Find Local Help" page to find a free assister near you.
  4. Until coverage starts, use a community health center for care. You can find one at findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov.

## Who it helps and what it costs

It helps anyone who loses job-based coverage, including family members on the worker's plan. An application is free. What you pay depends on income: some people qualify for Medicaid, others for a Marketplace plan with a tax credit. The enhanced tax credits ended after 2025, and KFF estimates that subsidized enrollees who kept the same plan saw premium payments rise 114% on average in 2026, so prices are higher than in recent years.

## Trade-offs and risks

- Missing the 60-day window can leave you uninsured until the next open enrollment.
- A new plan may have a different deductible and a different list of doctors than your old one.
- Savings depend on your income estimate. HealthCare.gov says to report a new job right away so you get the correct savings amount.
- In states that haven't expanded Medicaid, HealthCare.gov warns that some adults have fewer options and might not qualify for savings on a private plan.
