# Fraud and waste in government spending

Improper payments, fraud and waste in public programs, and how to cut them without hurting the people
the programs serve.

## The issue

Federal agencies reported about $186 billion in improper payments in fiscal year 2025, and GAO separately
estimates that fraud costs the federal government $233 billion to $521 billion a year. Money leaks out
when agencies pay without checking identity or eligibility first, rely on people's own word
(self-certification), or can't match their records against other agencies' data. Most improper payments are
overpayments, and the cause is often honest error, so cutting them means better checks, not just more
punishment. Taxpayers lose the money, and so do the people a program is meant to serve, when
payments go to the wrong place or when clumsy checks delay payments to eligible people.

## The solution

**Verify who gets a payment before the money goes out, and measure fraud and error honestly, so more of every public dollar reaches the people a program is meant to serve.**

1. **Congress and agencies:** run every payment and award through Treasury's free Do Not Pay checks, and
   fund the data-sharing and bank-account checks that are still "coming soon."
   ([evidence](solutions/do-not-pay-check-before-paying.md))
2. **Agencies and OMB:** assess fraud risk program by program using GAO's framework, putting prevention
   first, and collect the data GAO says is missing to measure fraud. ([evidence](solutions/gao-fraud-risk-framework.md))
3. **Congress, SSA and Treasury:** make a shared identity check (valid Social Security number, matching name
   and birth date, not deceased) standard before any large emergency program pays, with a fast appeal
   for real applicants, and keep the pandemic analytics center running.
   ([evidence](solutions/verify-identity-before-emergency-payments.md))
4. **Inspectors general and GAO:** keep one easy front door for tips, tell reporters when a tip was
   referred, and protect whistleblowers. ([evidence](solutions/report-suspected-fraud-to-the-right-watchdog.md))

## What we know

- **Improper payments are not the same as fraud.** GAO defines an improper payment as one that should not
  have been made or was made in the wrong amount. Fraud is obtaining something of value through willful
  misrepresentation. All fraudulent payments are improper, but not all improper payments involve fraud;
  waste and abuse are related but different. GAO says its fraud estimate is not comparable to the improper
  payment estimates. ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/improper-payments);
  [GAO-24-105833](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-105833))
- **Agencies reported about $186 billion in improper payments in fiscal year 2025.** Fifteen agencies
  reported it across 64 programs, up $24 billion from fiscal year 2024, and estimates since fiscal
  year 2003 total about $3 trillion. Most were overpayments. ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/improper-payments))
- **GAO estimates fraud costs $233 billion to $521 billion a year.** That is 3 to 7 percent of average federal
  obligations in fiscal years 2018 to 2022, from a statistical model (90 percent confidence). GAO warns the
  percentages should not be applied to single agencies or programs. OMB agreed with GAO's three
  recommendations on fraud measurement in general but disagreed with the estimate; they remain open.
  ([GAO-24-105833](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-105833))
- **Weak data and self-certification are named causes.** GAO points to poor data collection and use,
  eligibility verification gaps (especially in emergency programs) and reliance on self-certification,
  which it says should be avoided. ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/improper-payments))
- **Pandemic programs show the identity-check gap.** The Pandemic Response Accountability Committee (PRAC)
  sampled 662,000 identity records from 67.5 million funded applications in three programs and asked the
  Social Security Administration (SSA) to check them. SSA flagged nearly 24,000 as potentially fraudulent
  because the number was never issued or did not match the name or birth date. Extrapolated, PRAC
  estimates about $79 billion in potential identity fraud, which is potential, not confirmed, fraud.
  ([PRAC, June 2025](https://pandemicoversight.gov/news/articles/prac-issues-fraud-prevention-alert-pre-award-vetting-using-data-analytics-could-have-prevented-over-79-billion-in-potentially-fraudulent-pandemic-relief-payments))
- **Data analytics supports investigations.** As of September 30, 2026, PRAC's analytics platform, drawing
  on more than 60 data sources and over 1 billion records, had supported 1,340 pandemic-related
  investigations covering more than $2.6 billion in potential fraud, for more than 50 inspector general
  and law enforcement partners. ([PRAC](https://www.pandemicoversight.gov/advanced-data-analytics))
- **Checking before paying has measured results.** Treasury says it and Do Not Pay helped agencies
  prevent, detect and recover $11.7 billion in potential fraud and improper payments in fiscal year 2025.
  The service is free to federal agencies and federally funded, state-administered programs.
  ([Treasury Do Not Pay](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/dnp/))
