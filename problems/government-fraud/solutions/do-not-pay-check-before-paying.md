# Check before paying with Treasury's Do Not Pay and data matching

## Summary

Do Not Pay is a Treasury service that lets agencies verify identity and eligibility before an award or payment goes out. It is free to federal agencies and to federally funded, state-administered programs. Treasury reports it helped agencies prevent, detect and recover $11.7 billion in potential fraud and improper payments in fiscal year 2025.

## How it works

- The Bureau of the Fiscal Service runs Do Not Pay. Agencies reach it through a web portal, a system connection (Do Not Pay Connect), or a payment verification service, from manual lookup to direct integration.
- It checks payees against several kinds of risk: death records (including the full Death Master File), debts owed to government, exclusion lists (such as the List of Excluded Individuals and Entities and SAM exclusions), incarceration data, organization records, and bank account existence, status and ownership through the Account Verification Service.
- Treasury says screening awards and payments against Do Not Pay is required by federal law.
- Some data is still "coming soon" or limited to certain programs, including Numident (SSA identity data), SSA incarceration data and some identity validation. Several sources list limits, for example the IRS Publication 78 list has official names only, so some eligible organizations may be missing.
- PRAC's follow-up on deceased people's Social Security numbers found Do Not Pay supplied dates of death for 15,307 of 20,404 numbers, and called for better data sharing and more agency use of the system.

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** Congress and agencies can finish the missing data links (identity and incarceration data), require every state-administered federal program to screen before paying, and fund the staff time to clear flagged cases quickly. Treasury can publish match results by program so use can be measured.
- **People and companies with means:** Banks and payment processors can support account ownership checks so payments reach the named recipient. Researchers and philanthropies can fund independent studies of how often matches are wrong.
- **You, this week:** If you run or work for a program that receives federal money, ask your finance office whether it screens payees through Do Not Pay before paying, and use the contact on Treasury's page to learn how to enroll.

## Who it helps and what it costs

Taxpayers, and people who depend on programs, benefit when money does not go to deceased, excluded or wrongly identified payees. Use is free to agencies, so costs fall on agency staff time and system integration. Savings begin as soon as an agency checks before paying.

## Trade-offs and risks

- A match is a lead, not proof. Records can be wrong or out of date, and a wrongly flagged real recipient can see a payment delayed. Agencies need a quick way to review and correct matches.
- Matching works only as well as the underlying data. PRAC set aside records of people reported deceased later, because they may have been alive when they applied.
- Treasury's $11.7 billion is its own reported figure and includes detected and recovered amounts, not only payments stopped.
- Sharing personal data across agencies raises privacy risks that need access limits and audits.
