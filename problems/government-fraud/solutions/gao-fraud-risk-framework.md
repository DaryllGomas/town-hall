# GAO's fraud risk framework, prevention first

## Summary

GAO's Framework for Managing Fraud Risks in Federal Programs is a set of leading practices that agencies can use to find where fraud could happen and stop it early. It puts prevention first and asks agencies to keep measuring what works. GAO's own estimate of annual fraud losses shows why measurement matters.

## How it works

- The framework has components for control activities (prevent, detect and respond, with emphasis on prevention), structures and environmental factors that shape fraud risk, and ongoing monitoring and feedback.
- GAO built it from focus groups of antifraud professionals, interviews with federal inspectors general, foreign audit institutions and other experts, a literature review and validation by program officials.
- Agencies take a risk-based approach: assess where a program is exposed, design controls for those points, test them and adjust.
- On measurement, GAO estimated annual fraud losses of $233 billion to $521 billion (fiscal years 2018 to 2022). It made three recommendations: OMB, with the inspector general council, should develop guidance for collecting inspector general data and agency data to support fraud estimation, and Treasury, with OMB, should expand government-wide fraud estimation starting with higher-risk programs. As of GAO's page they were open.
- GAO also found that agencies found noncompliant with improper payment law must publish compliance plans, and OMB's revised Circular A-136 (July 2025) now directs agencies to say so in their financial statements.

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** Agency heads can run a fraud risk assessment for each program using the framework and publish the results. OMB can issue the data-collection guidance GAO recommended. Congress can oversee progress through hearings that use GAO's open recommendations list.
- **People and companies with means:** Foundations and universities can fund independent evaluations of which controls cut losses, and professional bodies can train program staff in the framework.
- **You, this week:** If you serve on a board or manage a grant, read GAO's framework page and ask your program staff which fraud risks they have assessed and how they would know a control works.

## Who it helps and what it costs

Taxpayers and program recipients benefit when agencies spot weak points before money leaves. The framework is free to use. The cost is staff time for assessments and data work. GAO's range is wide, so the first benefit is a better measure of the problem.

## Trade-offs and risks

- GAO says its range should not be applied to individual agencies or programs, and OMB disagreed with the estimate itself, so program-level targets need program-level data.
- Heavy controls can slow payments or burden eligible applicants. A risk-based approach means matching the strength of a check to the size of the risk.
- Assessments can become paperwork if no one acts on them.
