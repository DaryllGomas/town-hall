# Your first week after a layoff or hours cut

## Summary

The help that already exists after a job loss has deadlines and waiting periods, and people lose money by waiting. This is a short checklist of free programs to claim in the first days: unemployment benefits, health coverage, and the free job and training help at American Job Centers.

## How it works

**Day 1 to 2: file for unemployment.**
- File with the state where you worked, online, by phone or in person, as soon as you're out of work. You usually qualify if you lost the job through no fault of your own and earned enough in the "base period," roughly the first four of the last five completed calendar quarters. The first payment generally comes two to three weeks after you file, so every day of delay pushes it back.
- If your hours were cut rather than ending, ask your state agency about partial benefits, and ask your employer about a work-sharing plan (see the work-sharing fix in this folder).

**Day 2 to 5: keep health coverage.**
- Losing job-based coverage opens a 60-day Special Enrollment Period on HealthCare.gov or your state marketplace. You can enroll up to 60 days before or after losing coverage.
- Compare with COBRA, which keeps your old plan but at 100 percent of the premium plus a fee, and with Medicaid, which is based on your expected income for the year, not on whether you have a job.

**Day 3 to 7: go to an American Job Center.**
- Through state Rapid Response teams and local American Job Centers, laid-off workers can get career counseling, interview coaching, job search help, and access to retraining.
- Ask specifically about Dislocated Worker training funds and which training programs in your area have good placement results.

**Ongoing:** keep a simple log of every job you apply to (many states require it to keep benefits), and list any severance or final-pay dates so you know how long your money lasts.

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** States can let people file for unemployment and health coverage in one online step, send a checklist like this with every layoff notice, and cut the wait for the first payment.
- **People and companies with means:** Employers doing layoffs can hand every worker this checklist on the last day, pay for a few months of health premiums, give severance that bridges the two-to-three-week wait for benefits, and invite the state Rapid Response team on site before the last day. Philanthropists and community foundations can fund navigators who help people file.
- **You, this week:** Search "[your state] unemployment insurance apply" and file today, then check HealthCare.gov for a plan before your coverage ends. Both are free.

## Who it helps and what it costs

Anyone who lost a job or hours. It costs the worker nothing and takes a few hours. Unemployment is paid from state trust funds that employers pay into; marketplace subsidies and Medicaid are publicly funded. The main cost of skipping steps is lost money: benefits start later and a gap in coverage can mean large medical bills.

## Trade-offs and risks

- Unemployment rules, amounts and how long benefits last vary a lot by state, and some workers (many gig and self-employed workers, people fired for cause) may not qualify.
- Unemployment benefits replace only part of a paycheck.
- State systems can be slow; keep confirmation numbers and screenshots.
- American Job Center training funds often have income or layoff requirements, and quality varies by center.
