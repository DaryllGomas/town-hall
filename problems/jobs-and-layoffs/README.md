# Jobs, layoffs and low pay

## In people's words

> I was so happy to finally get a job, but they've cut everyone's hours in my department and now I live paycheck to paycheck again. Barely.

> 9 months ago, I got laid off from my job as a creative producer. Today, I work in fast food. How do I get out of this loop?

> I am NOT ok with the idea of having to work for 30 more years and living paycheck to paycheck the whole time!

## What we know

- **Minimum wage is far below what it costs to live.** In Texas, which uses the federal minimum of $7.25 an hour, MIT's Living Wage Calculator puts the 2026 living wage at $21.77 an hour for a single adult with no children and $35.77 for one adult with one child. ([MIT Living Wage Calculator, Texas](https://livingwage.mit.edu/states/48))
- **Millions want more hours than they get.** In September 2026, 4.5 million people worked part time for economic reasons: their hours were cut or they couldn't find full-time work. 7.1 million were unemployed (4.2 percent). ([BLS Employment Situation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm))
- **Layoffs are steady, not rare.** Employers laid off or discharged 1.6 million workers in August 2026 alone, a rate of 1.0 percent of jobs. ([BLS JOLTS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.nr0.htm))
- **Job searches are long for many.** In September 2026 the median unemployed person had been looking for 11.5 weeks, the average for 24.8 weeks, and 27.1 percent (1.9 million people) had been looking for 27 weeks or more. ([BLS table A-12](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.t12.htm))
- **Losing a long-held job often means a pay cut.** 3.3 million workers lost jobs they had held at least 3 years between 2023 and 2025. By January 2026, 66.1 percent were working again, but only about 49 percent of those back in full-time jobs earned as much as before, down from about 62 percent two years earlier. 44.4 percent lost their job because their position or shift was abolished. ([BLS Displaced Workers](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/disp.nr0.htm))
- **AI reaches a large share of jobs.** The IMF estimates about 60 percent of jobs in advanced economies may be affected by AI, with roughly half of those at risk of AI doing key tasks that lowers demand for workers. ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/Blogs/Articles/2024/01/14/ai-will-transform-the-global-economy-lets-make-sure-it-benefits-humanity))
- **Entry-level workers are feeling it first.** Using payroll data, Stanford researchers find employment of workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed occupations is 19 percent below where it would be had it kept pace with less-exposed peers; experienced workers show no comparable gap. ([Stanford Digital Economy Lab](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/))
