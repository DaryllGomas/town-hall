# A warm hand-off to a counselor at the moment money decisions happen

## Summary

Most people do not seek money help until something goes wrong, and many never find it. The idea is for banks, credit unions, employers and agencies to offer a booked appointment with a certified counselor or coach at the moments of strain, such as a missed payment, a denied loan or a job change, rather than a link or a brochure. It combines two things with evidence, coaching and nonprofit counseling, with a new way of reaching people.

## What is new

Existing programs wait for the person to call. This version builds the referral into the institution's own process: the lender, employer or office books the first session for the person, with consent, and follows up once. Whether this raises sign-up and outcomes has not been tested here; it is a proposal to pilot and measure.

## How it works

1. An institution picks a trigger it already sees, such as a late payment, an overdrawn account, a declined application or an enrollment into benefits.
2. It offers a short, no-pressure message with a booked appointment with a nonprofit counselor or coach, by video chat or phone, at no charge to the person.
3. It records only whether the appointment was kept, and an independent evaluator compares outcomes such as delinquency and savings against people who were not offered it, as the CFPB-funded trial did for coaching.

Why it could work: the problem is widespread and bunched at the edges. In the Federal Reserve's 2025 report, 26% of adults without a high school degree could cover a $400 emergency with cash, against 81% with a bachelor's degree. Help that arrives when the shortfall is happening reaches people a classroom could not. The trial of coaching found measurable gains in savings, debt and credit scores for people offered it.

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** Regulators and state agencies can allow and encourage referrals, and fund counseling agencies to take them.
- **People and companies with means:** Banks, credit unions and employers can build the referral into their process and pay the agency. Foundations can pay for the pilot and the independent evaluation.
- **You, this week:** If a lender or employer offers a counseling referral, take it. If not, ask them for one, or book your own session at nfcc.org.

## Who it helps and what it costs

People who hit a rough patch and would not otherwise seek help, including middle-income households: the NFCS found households earning $25,000 to $75,000 face many of the same difficulties as lower-income ones. Cost is the counselor's time, paid by the institution or a funder. Cost per person is unknown and is a pilot question.

## Trade-offs and risks

A bank referral could feel like a sales pitch, so the counselor must be independent and the offer must not affect the customer's terms. Sharing data needs consent and clear limits. Counselor supply may not keep up. Some people will decline, and the pilot should measure that too.
