# Financial literacy

## The issue

Most adults were never taught how credit, debt and investing work: U.S. adults answered 47% of personal finance questions correctly in 2026, and the score has never topped 52%. Schools have only recently started to require the subject, and some requirements never reach students, so many people learn about money by making costly mistakes. The gap shows up in cash cushions: 26% of adults without a high school degree could cover a $400 emergency with cash, against 81% of those with a bachelor's degree. The strain also reaches the middle, where households earning $25,000 to $75,000 face many of the same problems as lower-income ones.

## The solution

**Everyone should learn how money works before it costs them, and get trustworthy one-on-one help when it already has.**

1. **States and school systems:** require a stand-alone personal finance course with trained teachers and a set curriculum, not a topic folded into other classes. ([evidence](solutions/required-stand-alone-personal-finance-course.md))
2. **Employers:** offer free one-on-one financial coaching to hourly and lower-wage staff, with a first session booked during work time. ([evidence](solutions/one-on-one-financial-coaching.md))
3. **Banks and credit unions:** book a nonprofit counselor appointment for customers at the moment of a missed payment or denied loan. ([evidence](solutions/money-help-at-the-moment-of-need.md))
4. **Philanthropy:** fund nonprofit credit counseling and coaching so people can be seen quickly, and pay for independent evaluation. ([evidence](solutions/free-nonprofit-credit-counseling.md))

## What we know

- **Financial knowledge is low and slipping.** In the 2026 TIAA Institute-GFLEC P-Fin Index, U.S. adults answered 47% of questions correctly, and the score has never exceeded 52%. Gen Z answered 38% correctly, women trail men by 6 percentage points, and understanding risk was weakest at 36%.
  ([GFLEC P-Fin Index](https://gflec.org/initiatives/personal-finance-index/))
- **Cushions shrank in 2024.** In the FINRA Foundation's National Financial Capability Study (more than 25,500 adults), 46% had set aside three months of living expenses, down from 53% in 2021, and 53% always paid their credit cards in full, down 6 points. Households earning $25,000 to $75,000 showed more strain and more expensive credit card use.
  ([FINRA Foundation, 2025](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/finra-foundation-releases-sixth-wave-national-financial-capability))
- **Retirement saving follows education.** 80% of college graduates have a retirement account, against 37% of adults with no college experience.
  ([FINRA Foundation, 2025](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/finra-foundation-releases-sixth-wave-national-financial-capability))
- **A $400 surprise is out of reach for many.** 63% of adults would cover it with cash. By education, that is 26% for those without a high school degree, 51% with a high school degree or GED, 59% with some college and 81% with a bachelor's degree or more. By race and ethnicity it is 40% for Black, 46% for Hispanic, 73% for White and 77% for Asian adults.
  ([Federal Reserve SHED data table](https://www.federalreserve.gov/consumerscommunities/sheddataviz/unexpectedexpenses-table.html))
- **Required courses can change credit outcomes.** Researchers from the Federal Reserve Board and universities studied mandates in Georgia, Idaho and Texas. By the third year, credit scores of young adults were higher by 10.89, 16.19 and 31.71 points, compared with states without mandates, and severe delinquency was lower.
  ([Urban, Schmeiser, Collins and Brown, 2015](https://www.montana.edu/urban/FINRA_Issue_Brief.pdf))
- **Design decides whether a mandate works.** About 39 states have passed a personal finance graduation requirement, and 13 allow embedding it in other courses. A 2025 study found stand-alone courses raised credit scores through age 34, while embedded approaches showed no measurable effect. Only 39% of students receive any instruction in states with embedded requirements.
  ([Education Week, 2026](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/more-states-require-personal-finance-but-does-it-actually-work/2026/06))
- **One-on-one coaching changed outcomes in a randomized trial.** Of 945 people in two programs, those offered coaching saved more or owed less than a control group: savings rose $1,187 and credit scores 21 points at The Financial Clinic in New York, and debt fell $10,644 at Branches in Miami.
  ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/financial-coaching-strategy-improve-financial-well-being/))

## Open questions

- Free, trustworthy resources for budgeting, debt, credit and investing basics.
- What has evidence of actually changing outcomes (not just awareness)? Courses and coaching have some; what about workplace programs and auto-enrollment?
- How do we reach people who are struggling, rather than people already doing fine?
- What does coaching or counseling cost per person, and who should pay?
