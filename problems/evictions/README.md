# Evictions

Renters losing their homes: eviction filings, the days before an eviction, landlord sell-offs and
tax-driven rent hikes, and what happens after (records that block future rentals, homelessness, kids
changing schools).

## In people's words

> Being evicted on Monday. Do me a favor and ask your gods to send me some divine energy to get through this weekend

> We're being evicted and trying to figure out our financial and living situation right now.

> You can be a perfect tenant with the 'nicest' landlord and get evicted because your rent won't cover the taxes

## What we know

- **About 3.6 million eviction cases are filed in a typical year.** Princeton's Eviction Lab estimates
  3.6 million filings in 2018 against 2.7 million households, close to the 2000–2018 annual average;
  nearly 6 in 100 renting households were threatened with eviction that year.
  ([Eviction Lab, 2022](https://evictionlab.org/new-eviction-data-2022/);
  [Eviction Lab](https://evictionlab.org/))
- **Children are the most likely to be evicted.** Linking 2007–2016 court records to Census data, the
  Eviction Lab found 7.6 million people a year lived in households threatened with eviction, including
  2.9 million children. Adults living with a child faced a filing rate of 10.4%, against 5.0% for adults
  without children. Black renters are 18.8% of all renters but face 51.1% of eviction filings.
  ([Eviction Lab, 2023](https://evictionlab.org/who-is-evicted-in-america/))
- **Filings are still high.** In the 48 cities and states the Eviction Lab tracks (home to about a third
  of U.S. renter households), landlords filed 1.23 million eviction cases in 2025, roughly one for every
  13 renter households. Filings were above their post-pandemic average in 14 of those 48 sites.
  ([Eviction Lab, 2026](https://evictionlab.org/ets-report-2025/))
- **Half of renters are stretched.** A record 22.6 million renter households, half of all renters,
  spent 30% or more of their income on housing and utilities in 2023, according to Harvard's Joint
  Center for Housing Studies. Landlords' operating costs also rose, including a 26% jump in insurance
  costs in 2023. ([NLIHC summary of the JCHS report](https://nlihc.org/resource/latest-state-nations-housing-report-finds-record-number-housing-cost-burdened-renters))
- **Property tax increases reach renters.** A Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia study of Berkeley,
  California found landlords passed through $0.50 to $0.89 of every $1 of a property tax increase to
  renters. ([Philadelphia Fed, 2025](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/consumer-finance/consumer-credit/property-tax-pass-through-to-renters-a-quasi-experimental-approach))
- **An eviction order causes lasting harm.** A 2024 study of New York City and Cook County (Chicago)
  using random assignment of judges found an eviction order increases homelessness and hospital visits
  and reduces earnings and access to credit in the following two years, and lowers credit scores longer
  term. ([Quarterly Journal of Economics, via Yale Cowles Foundation](https://cowles.yale.edu/research/eviction-and-poverty-american-cities))
- **The record follows people.** Tenant screening reports can include cases the renter won or that were
  dismissed, and can match the wrong person by name. Only California and Colorado seal eviction records
  from the start of the case; over twenty states and D.C. have narrower sealing rules.
  ([Eviction Lab, 2026](https://evictionlab.org/the-scarlet-e-confronting-the-life-altering-consequences-of-an-eviction/);
  [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/tenant-background-checks/review-your-rental-background-check/))
