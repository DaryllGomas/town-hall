# Grocery prices

## The issue

Food prices are still rising, though more slowly than overall prices: in the 12 months to September 2026 food at home rose 1.8% and all items 3.7%. Even so, in 2024 about 1 in 7 U.S. households (13.7%) was food insecure, and 18.4% of households with children were. Many households fall between programs: nearly half of food-insecure people have incomes too high for SNAP, and new SNAP work rules in 2026 came as enrollment fell by about 5 million. Families with children are hit hardest, and more than three-quarters of households that reported not having enough to eat said they could not afford more food.

## The solution

**Every family can afford enough food, and the help that exists reaches the people it was built for.**

1. **States:** run online screening, reminders and phone interviews so eligible households keep SNAP, and explain which activities count toward the work rule. ([evidence](solutions/check-snap-eligibility-and-apply.md))
2. **School districts and states:** offer free breakfast and lunch to every student through the Community Eligibility Provision, with state funds to cover any gap. ([evidence](solutions/free-school-meals-for-all-students.md))
3. **Grocers and states:** match SNAP dollars spent on fruits and vegetables, funded by state programs and store partners. ([evidence](solutions/double-up-food-bucks-produce-match.md))
4. **Counties, states and philanthropy:** pilot a phased monthly grocery credit for households just above the SNAP line, and measure the results. ([evidence](solutions/bridge-grocery-benefit-above-snap-line.md))
5. **Employers and health systems:** fund application help at clinics and workplaces, and give paid time to apply. ([evidence](solutions/check-snap-eligibility-and-apply.md))

## What we know

- **Grocery prices are rising more slowly than prices overall, but food away from home is up more.** In
  the 12 months to September 2026 the CPI's food-at-home index rose 1.8%, food away from home 3.5% and
  all items 3.7%. USDA's September 2026 outlook forecasts food-at-home prices up 2.4% for 2026 (range
  1.8% to 3.0%), after 2.3% in 2025.
  ([BLS CPI, September 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm);
  [USDA ERS Food Price Outlook](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-price-outlook/summary-findings))
- **About 1 in 7 households was food insecure in 2024.** USDA counted 13.7% of households (18.3
  million), or 47.9 million people including 14.0 million children. Among households with children the
  rate was 18.4%. Very low food security was 5.4% of households. ([USDA ERS key statistics, 2024](https://www.ers.usda.gov/topics/food-nutrition-assistance/food-security-in-the-us/key-statistics-graphics))
- **The yearly USDA food security survey has ended.** FRAC reports USDA discontinued the Current
  Population Survey food security supplement in September 2025, so the 2024 data is the last in that
  series. A Census Bureau survey (HTOPS) found about 18 million households, and 21% of households with
  children, reporting they sometimes or often lacked enough to eat in June 2025; more than
  three-quarters said they could not afford more food. FRAC cautions the survey has a small sample and
  low response rates.
  ([FRAC on Census HTOPS, 2026](https://frac.org/blog/census-bureaus-household-trends-survey-shows-high-food-insufficiency-among-families-with-children))
- **Many food-insecure people earn too much for SNAP.** Feeding America's Map the Meal Gap 2026 (2024
  data) finds nearly half of people experiencing food insecurity have incomes too high to qualify, an
  average meal costs $3.70 (from $2.56 to $6.28 by county) and the national food budget shortfall
  surpassed $33 billion. ([Feeding America, 2026](https://www.feedingamerica.org/research/map-the-meal-gap/overall-executive-summary))
- **Most eligible people already get SNAP, but rules changed in 2026.** USDA estimated 88% of eligible
  people took part in fiscal year 2022. Work requirements from the 2025 budget law took effect in 2026,
  reaching ages 55-64 and some parents; USDA data reported by local news show enrollment fell from 42.4
  million (September 2025) to 37.0 million (April 2026). ([Mathematica for USDA FNS](https://www.mathematica.org/publications/reaching-those-in-need-estimates-state-supplemental-nutrition-assistance-program-participation-2022);
  [KTVU, Aug 2026](https://www.ktvu.com/news/snap-fewer-recipients-after-new-rules-implemented))
- **Free school meals reach 27.6 million students.** In 2025-26, 55,362 schools use the Community
  Eligibility Provision (74.9% of eligible schools), but growth was the slowest in the program's
  history, and FRAC warns the 2025 SNAP cuts could reduce schools' ability to qualify.
  ([FRAC CEP report, 2026](https://frac.org/cep-report-2026))
