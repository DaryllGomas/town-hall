# Check SNAP eligibility and apply, with help from a local office or food bank

## Summary

SNAP (food stamps) puts a monthly benefit on an EBT card, and most people who qualify already get it:
USDA estimates 88% of eligible people took part in fiscal year 2022. The people still missing out are
worth finding, and the rules changed in 2026, so a household that was turned down or dropped before
may be worth rechecking. Checking takes about as long as a phone call.

## How it works

- Your state sets the details, using income limits and the money you have in the bank. You apply with
  your state or local SNAP office, online, in person or by mail depending on where you live. Some
  applicants must complete an interview before approval.
- USDA keeps a state directory that links to each state's office and application. Search "SNAP state
  directory USDA" and pick your state.
- In 2026 the federal work rules widened. Many adults must work, volunteer or train for at least 80
  hours a month, or they are limited to three months of benefits in a three-year period. The age range
  grew from 18-54 to include ages 55-64 and some parents, and exemptions for veterans, people
  experiencing homelessness and young adults who aged out of foster care were removed. Ask the
  caseworker which rules apply to you and which activities count; a food bank or legal aid office can
  often explain this.
- Enrollment fell from 42.4 million people (September 2025) to 37.0 million (April 2026), per USDA data
  reported by local news. Some of that drop is people who found they no longer qualify, and some may be
  paperwork problems that can be fixed.

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** States can run online screening tools, text reminders before recertification, and
  phone interviews, so that eligible people don't lose benefits over missed paperwork. They can also
  publish plain-language guidance on which activities count toward the work rule.
- **People and companies with means:** Employers can post the state application link and let staff
  take paid time for the interview. Philanthropists and health systems can fund application assistance
  at food banks, clinics and libraries, where many people already ask for help with food.
- **You, this week:** Look up your state's SNAP office through the USDA directory (or call 211) and
  start an application now. You can apply even if you're unsure you qualify, and the office decides.
  If you were denied or dropped in the past year, apply again if your income or household has changed.
  If your household includes someone 55 to 64 or a parent who now falls under the work rule, ask what
  proof of hours is needed. Also ask your local food bank for help with the form.

## Who it helps and what it costs

- Helps low-income families, older adults and workers with low pay. For the household, applying is
  free.
- The benefit is paid by the federal government (about $95.8 billion in fiscal year 2025 for 42.4
  million people), with states sharing administration costs.
- Approval takes time, so food-bank help and school meals may still be needed in the meantime.

## Trade-offs and risks

- Nearly half of people who are food insecure have incomes too high to qualify for SNAP, so this
  doesn't help everyone.
- The work rule can end benefits for people who can't find 80 hours of qualifying activity or who
  can't document it, even if they are in need.
- Rules and state practices differ and keep changing; check your state's page, not a general summary.
