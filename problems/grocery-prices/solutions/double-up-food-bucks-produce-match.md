# Produce matching for SNAP shoppers (Double Up Food Bucks)

## Summary

Double Up Food Bucks matches what SNAP shoppers spend on fruits and vegetables, so each benefit dollar
buys more of them. In Michigan it matches dollar for dollar at more than 240 grocery stores, farmers
markets and corner stores. The best study found small gains that faded when the match ended, so it
stretches a food budget but is not a fix on its own.

## How it works

- A shopper pays for produce with a Michigan Bridge Card (SNAP) and gets matching Double Up dollars to
  spend on more fruit and vegetables. Current promotions let a shopper earn up to $50 a day in Double
  Up, and frozen fruits and vegetables without added salt, sugar or fat also qualify.
- Anyone with a Bridge Card is automatically eligible. The nonprofit Fair Food Network runs the program,
  funded by the State of Michigan, federal support and local partners.
- Evidence: a 2017 peer-reviewed study in BMC Public Health used scanner data from one Detroit
  supermarket and compared SNAP with non-SNAP customers. While the incentive ran, SNAP customers'
  fruit and vegetable spending rose by $0.40 a month on average (mostly vegetables), their share of
  spending on produce rose 0.7 percentage points, and they bought a little more variety. After it ended,
  the gains disappeared. Only 1.87% of SNAP transactions used a Double Up card, which the authors say
  limits conclusions.
- The authors suggested lowering the $10 purchase requirement and dropping a Michigan-grown rule.

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** States can fund produce matching and pay stores to run it. Counties and cities can
  support farmers markets and corner stores with the card-reader equipment they need to take part.
- **People and companies with means:** Grocers can offer a match on produce at checkout for SNAP
  shoppers. Foundations, health systems and employers can fund match dollars, since eating more produce
  is also a health goal.
- **You, this week:** If you live in Michigan and have a Bridge Card, use the "Find a Location" page at
  doubleupfoodbucks.org and shop there. Elsewhere, ask your farmers market or grocery store whether it
  matches SNAP dollars for produce; many local programs exist and your SNAP office or 211 may know.

## Who it helps and what it costs

- Helps SNAP households who want more fresh and frozen produce for the same benefit.
- Costs are the match dollars and program staff. Dollar amounts weren't found in the sources, so
  funders should ask the program for current figures.
- Works the moment a shopper uses it, but only while the match is running.

## Trade-offs and risks

- The study found effects were modest and not sustainable without the incentive.
- Low use limits the effect. People need to know about it and live near a participating store.
- The study covered one store in a Detroit community and a short period, so results may differ
  elsewhere.
