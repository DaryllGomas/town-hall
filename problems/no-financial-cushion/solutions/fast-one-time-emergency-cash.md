# Fast, one-time emergency cash before a crisis turns into an eviction

## Summary

For someone with no savings, one missed paycheck can snowball into late fees, an eviction filing and
losing their home. Local emergency financial assistance pays a one-time amount, usually straight to the
landlord or utility, at the moment of crisis (the Santa Clara program, for example, pays rent to
landlords and other household expenses such as utilities). Two rigorous studies found it sharply reduces
homelessness, and the benefits exceed the cost. Before the pandemic, 93% of communities had a help line
to call for this kind of help (Phillips and Sullivan, citing 211.org); the funds behind them need to
be faster, better known and better funded.

## How it works

1. A person who is behind on rent or a utility bill calls 211 or a local agency (a United Way, a
   Catholic Charities office, a community action agency) and asks for emergency financial assistance.
2. A caseworker checks that the household is at risk of losing housing and that the help will be
   enough to stabilize it.
3. The fund pays the landlord, utility or other bill directly, usually a one-time amount.

The evidence:

- **Santa Clara County, California (randomized trial).** People at imminent risk of homelessness were
  offered temporary financial assistance averaging nearly $2,000. It reduced homelessness by 3.8
  percentage points from a base rate of 4.1%, and the authors estimate the benefits exceed the costs
  (Phillips and Sullivan, University of Notre Dame, 2023).
- **Chicago (homelessness prevention call center).** Comparing callers on days when funds were available
  with days when they weren't, those who got help were 76% less likely to enter a shelter within six
  months, and 88% less likely among lower-income callers. The estimated cost per homeless spell averted
  was $10,300 (Evans, Sullivan and Wallskog, Science, 2016).

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** Cities, counties and states can fund standing emergency assistance pools that don't run
  dry mid-year, pay within days rather than weeks, and let people apply by phone or text with simple
  paperwork. Courts can refer every tenant at an eviction filing to the fund.
- **People and companies with means:** This is one of the most direct, measurable gifts available. A
  local donor or company could endow a year-round fund for their own county, for example committing to
  cover every eligible household that calls, and pay for an independent evaluation like the ones above.
  Landlords with large portfolios can agree to accept fund payments and pause filings while an
  application is pending. Employers can add a no-questions emergency grant for their own staff.
- **You, this week:** If you're behind or about to be, dial 211 (or visit 211.org) now, before an eviction
  notice, and ask specifically for "emergency rental assistance" or "emergency financial assistance" in
  your county. Have your lease, the amount owed and proof of income ready.

## Who it helps and what it costs

It helps renters and families who are one shock away from losing housing: in October 2025, 23% of
renters had been behind on rent at some point in the past year. The cost is the payment itself
(around $2,000 on average in the Santa Clara trial) plus staff. The 211 network reported more than 19
million referrals in 2025. It works in days to weeks, which is what matters when rent is due.

## Trade-offs and risks

- Funds often run out, and waiting lists defeat the purpose. Steady funding matters more than one-time
  drives.
- It's hard to predict who would lose housing without help, so some money goes to households that would
  have found a way anyway. Both studies still found the benefits outweigh the costs.
- In the Chicago study, assistance had no statistically significant effect for higher-income callers;
  targeting matters.
- One payment doesn't fix low pay. It buys time to get stable, and works best paired with the other
  fixes in this folder.
