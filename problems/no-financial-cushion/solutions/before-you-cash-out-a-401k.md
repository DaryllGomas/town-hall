# Before cashing out a 401(k) for rent, use the cheaper options first

## Summary

When a layoff or a cut in hours hits, cashing out a whole 401(k) can feel like the only way to keep the
rent paid. It is usually the most expensive way: 20% is withheld up front, the rest is taxed as income,
and a 10% additional tax may apply. Several existing options, from unemployment insurance to a
penalty-free $1,000 emergency withdrawal, can cover the gap while keeping most of the retirement money
intact.

## How it works

Work through these in order, taking only what the gap needs:

1. **File for unemployment the day you're laid off.** Each state runs its own program. You file with the
   state where you worked, and the first payment usually takes about 2 to 3 weeks, so filing early
   matters. Eligibility generally requires losing work through no fault of your own.
2. **Call 211 for rent, utility and bill help.** 211 is a free, confidential line run with local United
   Ways that connects people to local help with housing, utility and food costs. Local emergency
   rent funds can be a far cheaper bridge than a cash-out (see the related fix
   [fast-one-time-emergency-cash.md](fast-one-time-emergency-cash.md)).
3. **Take the $1,000 emergency withdrawal, not the whole account.** Since 2024, the tax code lets you
   take one "emergency personal expense" distribution per calendar year, up to $1,000 (or the vested
   balance above $1,000, if less), from a qualified plan or IRA without the 10% additional tax. It is
   still taxed as income. Ask your plan whether it offers this.
4. **If more is needed, ask about a hardship distribution for the exact amount.** Plans may (but don't
   have to) allow these for listed needs, including preventing eviction or foreclosure, medical care and
   funeral costs. It is limited to the amount needed. It is taxed, may carry the 10% additional tax,
   and can't be repaid.
5. **Leave the rest in the plan or roll it over directly.** A direct rollover to an IRA or new employer's
   plan avoids the 20% withholding. If a check is paid to you, you have 60 days to roll it over.

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** The IRS and Department of Labor could require plans to show the $1,000 emergency
  option and the costs of a full cash-out on every separation notice and withdrawal screen, in plain
  numbers. States could include a "before you cash out" page in every unemployment claim confirmation.
- **People and companies with means:** Employers can switch on the $1,000 emergency distribution and
  hardship options in their plans, and have HR walk every laid-off worker through this list before their
  last day. Recordkeepers can put a tax-cost calculator in front of the "cash out" button. Philanthropists
  can fund free financial coaching for people in the first month after a layoff.
- **You, this week:** Before you request any withdrawal, call your plan's phone number and ask three
  questions: Do you offer the $1,000 emergency personal expense distribution? Do you offer hardship
  distributions for eviction? What will I actually receive after withholding if I cash out? Then file for
  unemployment and call 211.

## Who it helps and what it costs

It helps anyone with retirement savings who is facing a short-term gap, especially people who have just
been laid off. That is a lot of people: about 6% of eligible Vanguard plan participants took a hardship
withdrawal in 2025, mostly (for those earning under $100,000) to avoid eviction or pay medical bills. It costs nothing to use. The savings can be large: on a full cash-out, withholding,
income tax and the possible 10% additional tax all come out of the money that would otherwise keep
growing for retirement. Unemployment and 211 help can take days to weeks, so start immediately.

## Trade-offs and risks

- $1,000 won't cover a month's rent in many places; it is a bridge, not a fix.
- Not every plan offers the emergency or hardship options. Ask; don't assume.
- Hardship distributions can't be put back, so retirement still takes a hit.
- Waiting for unemployment or rent help while bills come due is stressful, and local funds run out.
  Applying early is the best defense.
- This is general information, not tax advice. Your plan or a tax preparer can confirm your numbers.
