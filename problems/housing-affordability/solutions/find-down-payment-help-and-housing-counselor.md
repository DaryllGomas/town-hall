# Find down-payment help and a HUD-approved housing counselor this week

## Summary

Saving a down payment is one of the biggest walls for first-time buyers, yet about 2,600 down-payment
and homeownership programs exist across the U.S., with an average benefit of about $18,000. Many are
not limited to low incomes or to first-time buyers. A HUD-approved housing counselor can help you find
the ones you qualify for.

## How it works

- **The gap.** First-time buyers were a record-low 21% of buyers in the year to June 2025, and their
  median age reached a record 40. Their median down payment was 10%, the highest since 1989. They paid
  mainly from savings (59%), financial assets (26%) and gifts or loans from family and friends (22%),
  so people without family money start behind (National Association of Realtors, 2025).
- **The help.** Down Payment Resource, which tracks these programs, counted 2,619 homeownership
  programs at the end of 2025; 1,941 (74%) help with down payments or closing costs. The average
  benefit was about $18,000. More than half (53%) of down-payment programs forgive part or all of the
  help over time. They are run by states, local governments and nonprofits.
- **Who qualifies.** 980 programs accept repeat buyers, 246 waive the first-time rule for service
  members and veterans, 62% have income limits above $100,000 and 10% have no income limit.
- **The guide.** The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau says many states and local organizations
  offer down-payment and closing-cost help, and that a HUD-approved housing counseling agency can
  connect you to the programs in your area. HUD keeps a searchable list of these agencies.
- **The order of steps:** meet a counselor, ask whether your programs require a homebuyer class,
  check your credit, get a list of programs you qualify for, then pick a lender that works with
  your state housing finance agency's programs.

## Who can make it happen

- **Government:** State housing finance agencies and cities can put every program into one plain
  search page, accept one shared application, and fund more counselors. HUD can keep funding housing
  counseling grants. Lenders that sell loans to the federal mortgage agencies could be asked to tell
  every first-time applicant about local programs.
- **People and companies with means:** Employers can offer down-payment matching or forgivable loans
  for workers who buy near the job. Banks and credit unions can train loan officers on local programs
  and offer them at application. Philanthropists can fund grants for first-generation buyers whose
  families have no savings to pass on.
- **You, this week:** Use HUD's housing counselor search (linked below) to find an approved agency
  near you and book a first meeting; ask which down-payment programs fit your income, county and the
  price range you're looking at. Then search your state name plus "housing finance agency" and read
  its homebuyer page. Never pay an upfront fee to anyone promising a "grant"; real programs are run
  through agencies and approved lenders.

## Who it helps and what it costs

- Helps renters with steady income who could afford a monthly payment but not the upfront cash,
  especially first-generation buyers, service members and veterans.
- Ask the counseling agency about any fee when you book; the help itself is paid by state and local budgets, bond
  programs, federal grants and some employers. A buyer can start this week; closing takes months.

## Trade-offs and risks

- Down-payment help raises buying power but doesn't add homes. Where supply is tight, it can push
  prices up unless paired with building more homes.
- Many programs are second loans that must be repaid when you sell or refinance, or are forgiven only
  if you stay a set number of years. Read the terms.
- Funds can run out, and program limits on price may not match local markets.
- Buying stretches some households too far; a good counselor will also tell you when to wait.
